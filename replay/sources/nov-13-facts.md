# November 13, 2026 — the documented record (our notes, sources cited)

Written 2026-11-14 from firsthand reads of the sources below. Times are
Eastern (ET = UTC-5 on that date) unless marked UTC.

- Early afternoon: the FAA began identifying instability in radar and
  communications systems at Air Route Traffic Control Centers; officials
  later blamed aging telecommunications routers that became overloaded
  with data. (Reuters reporting, Nov 13-14)
- 3:54 p.m. ET (20:54 UTC): the FAA announced a nationwide ground stop —
  all departures at all U.S. airports held — after communication issues
  affected multiple Air Route Traffic Control Centers. The advisory text
  is preserved in faa-advisory-2026-11-13.txt.
- During the stop: more than 3,500 aircraft were airborne when it was
  issued; fewer than 400 remained in the sky by 8 p.m. ET. The National
  Weather Service also reported nationwide internet and communications
  issues the same day. (Contemporaneous reporting)
- 8:15 p.m. ET (01:15 UTC, Nov 14): the FAA lifted the ground stop and
  departures resumed, after the FAA tested its systems. The stop lasted
  more than four hours — the longest such stop in 25 years, and only the
  second nationwide ground stop since September 11, 2001 (the first was
  January 11, 2023, a NOTAM system failure).
- Scale: Friday closed with thousands of cancellations (tallies vary by
  source and hour: Reuters cited FlightAware counts of more than 1,000
  cancellations and nearly 10,000 delays during the stop; a later
  FlightAware day tally reported 9,159 cancellations and 7,190 delays;
  FlightRadar24 counted more than 6,600 cancellations and 8,600 delays).
  On Saturday morning, airlines had cancelled more than 3,500 further
  flights working through the backlog. The FAA expected operations to
  return to normal by November 15. No sign of a cyberattack was found,
  per the Homeland Security Secretary.
- Aftermath: the President declared updating the national air traffic
  control system "a national emergency." Congress had approved $12.5
  billion the prior year for ATC modernization; a further $30 billion
  was being sought.
